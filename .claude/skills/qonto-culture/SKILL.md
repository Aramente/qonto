# Qonto Culture Reference

Source-grounded snapshot of how Qonto talks about itself, how it organizes work, and how it grows people. All claims are sourced — see footnotes. Anything not publicly documented is marked as such; do not invent.

## Values (Official)

Qonto operates with **five values**, displayed in this order on the official culture page. Each value pairs a tagline with concrete behaviors. Quote them verbatim when writing for employees. [^1]

1. **Customer Focus — "Prioritize customers in everything you do"**
   - Put yourself in customers' shoes.
   - Aim at delivering both quality *and* speed.
   - Prioritize customers over processes.

2. **Ownership — "Own your part, get things done"**
   - Say what you do, and do what you say.
   - Your job is not done until *THE* job is done.
   - Fix problems when you see them (the "broken window" principle).

3. **Teamwork — "Make (team)work easy"**
   - Play together toward the same goals.
   - Treat colleagues as if they were customers.
   - Be an energy giver.

4. **Mastery — "Continuously raise the bar"**
   - Dare to experiment and learn from it.
   - Repeating tasks is an opportunity to get better, more efficient, more sustainable.
   - Challenge old habits, fight misconceptions.

5. **Integrity — "Always do what's right, and respect people"**
   - Don't mess with customers' money and data, nor with Qonto's.
   - Design inclusivity in.
   - Use critical thinking constructively.

**Historical note**: Qonto previously listed "Ambition" as a top-level value. As the company scaled, leadership found Ambition was "no longer actionable for most teams" and rephrased it as concrete behaviors — primarily **"ruthlessly customer-first"** and **"taking ownership"**. The current five-value set is the result of that revision. [^1][^7]

## Voice & Tone

### Bilingual operating reality
- English is the working language across product, engineering, and most cross-office collaboration. French surfaces in recruiting copy, French-market marketing, and informal internal speech.
- Public Medium posts are almost all in **English**. Public careers copy ships in **both FR and EN**, with the FR version slightly more lyrical.

### Register
- **Confident, mission-anchored, slightly hortative.** Sentences are short. Quotes from leaders carry the philosophy; the surrounding prose stays clean.
- **No corporate-speak hedging.** Qonto does not say "we strive to" or "we believe that"; it says "we do" or quotes a person saying "I want…".
- **Manager-as-coach framing.** Managers are described as people who "set up the team for success," "help the team succeed on the right topics," not as people who direct or oversee.
- **Lean/Toyota vocabulary is real, not decorative.** Kaizen, Jidoka, Just-in-Time, Andon, Pull-System, value streams — these are operating words inside Qonto, not buzzwords. Use them when accurate; don't sprinkle them. [^3][^4]

### Signature phrasings — English
- "Qontoer" (noun) — what employees call themselves. [^9]
- "The Qonto Way" — the cultural-strategic framework, used as a proper noun. [^2]
- "Energize entrepreneurs" — the founder-mission framing. [^2]
- "Get finance out of their way — for real." [^5]
- "Finance is not strategy: it is a hidden tax." [^5]
- "Everything else can change. This cannot." (re: customer obsession) [^5]
- "Talent is not something we have but something we grow." [^4]
- "Learn lasting skills in the fast lane." [^8]
- "Go far by going together." [^8]
- "Take pride in great work." [^8]

### Signature phrasings — French
- "Apprentissage éclair" — Lightning Learning. [^10]
- "Unis vers l'excellence" — United Toward Excellence. [^10]
- "Qualité garantie" — Quality Guaranteed. [^10]
- "Osez dépasser vos limites." — Dare to exceed your limits. [^10]
- "Cassent les codes sans se marcher dessus." — Break the rules without stepping on each other. [^10]
- "Exprimez pleinement qui vous êtes." — Fully express who you are. [^10]
- "Voyez votre impact se concrétiser immédiatement." — See your impact materialize immediately. [^10]

### What Qonto doesn't say
- No "passionate about X."
- No "we're a family."
- No "rockstars / ninjas / wizards."
- No "synergy," "leverage," "best-in-class."
- Avoid "agile" as a positive identifier — Qonto explicitly distances itself from sprint-based agile, calling sprints "fundamentally a greedy algorithm" and preferring lean. [^11]

## Performance Review Approach

### Cadence
**Bi-annual Progress Reviews.** Every employee has a structured conversation with their manager twice a year covering career trajectory, growth opportunities, and feedback on any aspect of the experience. [^12]

### Framework — The Qonto Way (three pillars)
Managers are evaluated and operate against three pillars, used as the scaffolding for development conversations: [^11][^6]
1. **Boosting Individual Performance** — "Help each team member become the best version of themselves, and once that's done, start over and help them become the next best version."
2. **Catalyzing Collective Performance** — "A collection of smart individuals achieves great results only when they organize efficiently to complement each other."
3. **Building Up the Team** — "Welcoming the right people who will reinforce the culture, live by the values, and ultimately make their team greater than the sum of its members."

### Levels & promotion mechanics
- Position levels are assigned during hiring via a **skill matrix** assessment (hard + soft skills), then re-assessed at Progress Reviews. [^12]
- **Salary grid** anchors fairness across teams; the skill matrix gives it coherence. [^12]
- **Dual track**: expert (IC) and manager paths exist with mobility between them. [^12]
- Last cycle outcome: **62% of employees got a promotion or salary increase** across the five offices. For Product specifically: 35% raises, 15% promotions. [^12]

### What gets evaluated (engineering example)
Emma Zamia (CTO) describes engineering progression using **"structured frameworks" of successive mastery levels** within three dimensions: individual development, collective organization, and cultural alignment to the values. [^11] No public IC1–IC6-style ladder has been disclosed.

### Feedback style
- "Right and obligation to identify and raise performance issues" — both up and down. [^6]
- Continuous, not just at review time — managers run "paired learning sessions" that evolve into problem-solving exercises. [^6]
- Andon culture: stop work, surface the abnormality, fix it before continuing. Applied to product/eng quality and treated as a trust-building mechanism: "Andon develops a strong bond between the management, which is committed to react and help, and the team: it's called Trust, with a capital T." [^4]

### What Progress Reviews are *not*
Not publicly documented: rating scales, calibration process, peer/360 component, written self-assessment templates. Assume bi-annual structured 1:1 + skill-matrix scoring unless told otherwise.

## Compensation Philosophy

- **Salaries benchmarked above the industry median.** Stated explicitly on careers pages in both FR and EN. [^8][^10]
- **ESOPs** for eligible roles. [^8]
- **Salary grid + skill matrix** drives fairness; same level → same band regardless of team. [^12]
- **No public information** on bonus structure, equity refresh policy, or geographic differentials.

### Benefits stack (Europe-wide)
- Hybrid (3 office / 2 remote per week) or fully-remote roles, depending on position.
- 12 bonus remote days per year (work from anywhere within ±3h CET).
- Private health insurance.
- Mental health: 4 sessions/year (subsidized).
- Subsidized fitness pass.
- Parental leave + disability support.
- MacBook, meal vouchers, 50%+ travel subsidy, referral bonuses, team-building budgets. [^8]

## Team Organization & Rituals

### Operating system: Lean, not Scrum
Qonto runs a Toyota-Production-System-derived operating model. Concrete practices in active use: [^3][^4]

- **Visual Management** — physical Kanban boards (not just SaaS), daily Success Meetings (15 min morning + 5–10 min afternoon).
- **Andon** — anyone can stop the line when quality drops; management is obligated to help diagnose and fix before resuming.
- **Pull-System** — strict WIP limits between teams (e.g., Product holds max two features in Tech's queue; new specs only enter when a slot frees).
- **Kaizen** — small cross-functional groups (e.g., 4 people, 30 min/day) running continuous problem-solving cycles. Used both for ops metrics and for *growing the people inside the cycle*.

### What "Success Meetings" replace
Not standups in the agile sense — they're alignment rituals tied to visible Kanban state. Morning sets the focus; afternoon checks on impediments.

### Cross-functional norms
- "Cross-functional feedback" and "regular alignment meetings" between functions are baseline expectations, not initiatives. [^7]
- "Treat colleagues as if they were customers" is the operating norm for internal collaboration. [^1]

### Hiring funnel (six stages)
1. HR Interview — career goals + mutual fit.
2. Hiring Manager Interview — skills + team fit.
3. Case Study + debrief — task-based, mimics the real work.
4. Qontoer Interview — peer interview (optional for ICs, mandatory for managers).
5. Final Interview — senior leadership fit.
6. Offer — 48h decision window.

Commitments: 48h response to application; 48h feedback after every stage; ~20 working days end-to-end. [^13]

## When designing for Qonto employees — checklist

- Lead with **one of the five values**, named verbatim. Don't invent new ones.
- If the surface is bilingual, write FR and EN as **siblings**, not translations — the French should feel like French, not translated English. The FR tone runs slightly more poetic (`osez`, `unis vers`, `voyez votre impact se concrétiser`).
- For manager-facing surfaces: anchor on the three pillars (individual / collective / team-building).
- For IC-facing surfaces: anchor on **Mastery** and **Ownership** — these carry the most operational weight day-to-day.
- For review/feedback surfaces: bi-annual Progress Review is the cadence; skill matrix + salary grid is the structure; "Boosting Individual Performance" is the framing.
- Avoid sprint/scrum/agile vocabulary. Prefer lean: kaizen, value stream, pull, andon, visual management — but only when accurate.
- Refer to employees as **Qontoers** in internal-tone copy.

## Named biases facilitators must catch
- **Recency** — only recent quarter matters.
- **Halo / horn** — one trait colors everything.
- **Central tendency** — everyone rated average to avoid hard conversations.
- **Affinity** — favoring those similar to the manager.
- **Gender / race-coded language** — "abrasive", "emotional", "aggressive", "not a culture fit".
- **Spotlight / visibility bias** — vocal / in-office contributors over heads-down / remote.
- **Idiosyncratic rater effects** — some managers just rate higher across the board.

## Facilitator role
- **Neutral**: NOT a manager with direct reports being discussed.
- Owns: time, ground rules, redirect when bias surfaces.
- Pre-loaded with intervention scripts so calls aren't accusatory.

## Intervention scripts (transferable to AI tooltip / hover prompts)
- "Before we lock this rating, let's check our bias list. Does anyone see potential recency effect or halo/horn influence here?"
- "What's the specific behavioral evidence over the full year?"
- "Walk us through one example from Q1 or Q2, not just recent quarters."
- "If [employee] worked remotely vs in-office, would this rating change? Why?"

## Structured bias checks
- **Distribution check**: are ratings distributed similarly across demographic groups? One group clustering low → investigate.
- **Recency check**: is one quarter overweighted?
- **Evidence check**: every rating backed by 2–3 specific examples spanning the cycle.

## Pre-meeting prep beats live deliberation
- Culture Amp's "Ratings Preview" + Confirm's AI-synthesized profiles both reduce live-meeting time by surfacing disagreements asynchronously.
- The live meeting should focus on the 15–20% of contested cases, not re-walk every employee.

## Anti-pattern: "secret handshake" calibrations
- Calibration outcomes not shared with employees, no documented rationale.
- Confirm calls this out as the failure mode their audit trail solves.
